# Dataset card: Tabrizi et al. (2020)

> Generated by `make inspect` (`scripts/inspect_dataset.py`) from the files in `data/raw/tabrizi2020/`. Do not edit by hand.

## Source

Sahar S. Tabrizi, Vajiheh Javani, Saeid Pashazadeh. *Data Acquired by a Single Object Sensor (IMU) of Table Tennis Forehand Strokes.* Mendeley Data, Version 3, 2020. DOI: [10.17632/b7bc9y232m.3](https://doi.org/10.17632/b7bc9y232m.3). Licence: CC BY 4.0.

- One BNO055 9-axis IMU on the racket face; 16 players (8 novice, 8 professional); Basic, Topspin and Push forehands.
- Strokes are shadow-play, without a ball (from the authors' papers).
- C1-C5 are five evaluation criteria. Each value is the average of 3 coaches; individual coach scores are not published.

## Sampling rate

- **Stated by the authors:** 100 Hz (Data in Brief article, 2020), 70 Hz (Computational Intelligence and Neuroscience paper, 2021).
- **Observed rows per whole second:** mean 8.27, std 3.60, min 1, max 24 (n = 2308 whole seconds). Timestamps have 1-second resolution, so this is the finest timing available; each file's first and last (partial) second are skipped, and 91 files whose timestamps stop at minutes are skipped.
- **Rows per stroke:** median 23, 5th-95th percentile 9-50, min 2, max 430.
- **Exact duplicate rows** (a row identical to the previous one in every channel): 7101 of 33194 consecutive pairs (21.4%). Per stroke: median 21.2%. Duplicates are kept.

## Signal characteristics (used set)

- **Peak rotation rate per stroke:** median 1.22 rad/s (70 deg/s), 95th percentile 3.02 rad/s, max 8.03 rad/s.
- **Acceleration magnitude, all rows:** median 9.81 m/s², 1st-99th percentile 8.79-11.44 m/s², max 25.26 m/s². Gravity alone is 9.81 m/s².
- **Peak on the first row:** 238 of 1344 strokes (17.7%) have their largest rotation rate on the first logged row.

**Interpretation (supported by these numbers, not certain):** a modest peak rotation rate and an acceleration magnitude that stays close to gravity, at about 8 rows per second, suggest that the logging rate undersamples the fast phase of the stroke. The signal then mostly reflects the racket's orientation rather than swing dynamics. Strokes whose peak is on the first row are a likely sign that recordings sometimes start mid-swing.

## Data checks

- Stroke labels: the scores CSV uses several spellings (e.g. `BASIC `, `Basic `, `Top`, `push`). After normalizing to BASIC, PUSH, TOPSPIN, 0 of 1570 labels disagree with the processed folder.
- Processed files are the raw rows zero-padded at the front: 1551 of 1570 match (1548 are 70 rows long, 22 are longer and not padded). Mismatches: 1223, 1300-1312, 1314-1318.
- For 19 of those ids (1223, 1300-1312, 1314-1318) the raw file has accelerometer and gyroscope zero in every row, while the processed file holds different rows with non-zero accelerometer and gyroscope data and a different first timestamp. Following the approved rule (rows come from the raw files), these samples are excluded as zero-sensor files; the processed versions are not used.
- Player overrides: id 720 -> P05 (scores CSV says P06; the spreadsheet range 651-720 says P05).
- Player codes: the CSV writes `NO1`/`PO1` (letter O); the spreadsheets write `#N01`/`#P01`. Both are normalized to `N01`/`P01`.
- Recording dates (from the timestamps): 2019-09-17, 2019-09-19, 2019-09-22, 2019-09-25.
