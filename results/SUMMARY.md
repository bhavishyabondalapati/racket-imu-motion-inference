## Limitations

- Table tennis rather than tennis, forehands only, and shadow strokes without a ball (confirmed by the authors' papers).
- No synchronized video or motion capture, so body motion cannot be inferred or validated.
- 16 players (15 with usable accelerometer and gyroscope data), one sensor placement on the racket face.
- A very low observed logging rate (about 8 rows per second versus 100 Hz / 70 Hz stated); the signal probably undersamples the fast phase of the stroke and mostly reflects racket orientation.
- Classification relies on static racket angle: channel means alone match the full models; a recording-block artifact cannot be ruled out because each stroke type was recorded in its own block.
- Level is fully confounded with recording day: all novices were recorded on 2019-09-25, all pros on other days. Novice-vs-pro accuracy (D5) may partly reflect session differences such as sensor mounting.
- Coach scores are averages of 3 coaches per criterion, so coach agreement (a ceiling for quality prediction) cannot be computed. Scores depend strongly on level.
- Temperature scaling fitted on 2 inner validation players did not transfer to unseen players; confidences are uncalibrated.
- Choosing the 'best' model by its LOPO score (for D4-D6) is a small optimistic bias.

## Future work

- Collect synchronized racket-IMU and video or motion-capture recordings, at a higher logging rate and with stroke types interleaved within recordings, to extend this from stroke recognition to body-motion inference and to rule out recording-block artifacts.
